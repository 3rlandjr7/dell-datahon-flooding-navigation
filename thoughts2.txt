ways to improve for real public use

    take gps photos during times of flooding to compare vs times of not flooding in major metropolitan areas

    use percent comparisions for roadways instead of whole pictures (and train on roadway data instead of whole picture)

    implement the heatmap to naviagtion software with a "flood mode" where the naviagation would try to avoid darkest areas (maybe darkness threshold changes based on current weather?)

    other option is to use an openstreetmaps approach and have people volunteer flooding information for their streets along with a gauge of how bad it is currently raining, and use those 2 pieces of information to develop a flood score for those coordinates.






what the process looked like
    qwen graded it's thoughts for flooding in before/after photos

    we looked into 75 photos to get human input to find inaccuracies in qwen and test fixes for the grading prompt

    used our graded photos to develop heuristics (general rules for grading) such as
        - green before and flat brown after = floodwater, not dirt (plowed fields and construction show texture, floodwater is flat and smooth)
        - ground under trees and buildings counts as flooded when water surrounds them, the water just didn't reach the treetops or roofs
    these guided our own grading. we tested them in qwen's prompt too, but they didn't help: showing qwen both photos at once made it read the camera color change as "green turned brown" and call almost everything flooded

    qwen's biggest mistake was reading muddy water that fills the whole photo as bare dirt, scoring flooded tiles as 0%. we fixed it with a second check: for tiles with no new flood, qwen is asked a yes/no question about standing floodwater, and if it says yes it estimates the water percent again with a note that floodwater is present (show example: photo #70, qwen first said 0%, after the check 95%, we graded it 100%)

    result on photos held back for testing: qwen agrees with us on flooded vs dry 91% of the time (up from 83%), but its percentages are still off by about 19 points on average




final qwen prompts (each sent with one aerial photo)

    pass 1, for every before and after photo:

This is an overhead aerial photo of a ~530 m square area in the Houston, Texas region.
Estimate what percent of the image is covered by water of any kind: rivers, ponds, reservoirs, or floodwater.
Floodwater from the air is usually flat, opaque, muddy brown, tan or grey-brown with no texture,
with tree crowns, rooftops and road edges sticking out of it.
Answer with a single integer from 0 to 100 and nothing else.

    new floodwater = after photo % minus before photo %

    pass 2a, yes/no check on the after photo, only for tiles with no new flood:

Is any of the ground in this overhead aerial photo covered by standing floodwater?
Floodwater can be brown, tan, grey or dark, and can fill the whole frame so that only trees,
roofs and road edges show. Bare soil, dirt fields and shadows are not water. Answer yes or no.

    pass 2b, if the check says yes: this note, followed by the pass 1 prompt, on the after photo:

This photo was taken just after Hurricane Harvey and contains standing floodwater.
Muddy water filling most of the frame is water, not bare soil.
