What this map can get wrong

  • Brown floodwater over fields can read as bare ground. Even after the recheck pass, 4 of 11 hand-checked tiles scored 0% were flooded.
  • Estimated percentages run about 19 points off the hand-graded value on average, so routing uses thresholds, not exact numbers.
  • Roads outside a photographed tile have no flood data at all. The route card says how much of your trip that is.
  • This is imagery from 2017, for a demo. Do not drive by it.

How we built it

  1. Start with the imagery

    NOAA flew aerial photos of the Texas coast after Hurricane Harvey made landfall in August 2017. We paired each after photo with a before photo of the same ground, about 1.6 GB in all.

  2. Cut it into tiles

    575 tiles, each with its own bounds and center from the dataset's image_metadata.csv. 267 of them fall in the west Houston / Addicks–Barker area, which is the area this page routes on.

  3. Ask a vision model how much water it sees

    Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, served with vLLM on the Dell Pro Max GB10, estimated the share of each photo covered by water. New floodwater is the after percentage minus the before percentage, floored at zero.

  4. Grade it by hand

    We hand-labeled four sets of tiles as flooded or dry, and kept two of those sets held out so we were not tuning against our own answers.

  5. Find the failure

    Whole flooded tiles came back 0%. Muddy brown floodwater over fields and streets does not look like water to the model — it looks like bare ground.

  6. Fix it, part one: ask a yes/no question first

    For every tile scored 0%, we asked a separate question — is there floodwater in this photo, yes or no? 90 tiles went through that check.

  7. Fix it, part two: re-score the ones that said yes

    On a yes, we scored the after photo again with a note telling the model that muddy brown water is still water. 39 tiles came back with a new, higher score. Showing both photos at once was tried and dropped: the model read the camera's color change between flights as flooding.

  8. Check it against the held-out sets

    Flooded-versus-dry agreement on the held-out tiles rose from 83% to 91%. The percentages themselves are still loose, so we never present them as measurements.

  9. Route on it

    Every road segment takes the worst class of the tiles it touches. Above 50% new floodwater is impassable; 25–50%, and anything the model never scored, costs six times its length. The cheapest path wins, and we run a second, flood-blind search so the map can show you what was avoided.

Built for the UTSA × Dell GB10 Datathon, November 2026. Model: Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct via vLLM on a Dell Pro Max GB10.
